How Iradi horses are named

Names in Tukum are written as the studbooks wrote them. Both registries follow much the same rules for a new name, and the State Hippodrome takes a horse's name from its certificate, so a name refused by the studbook cannot be raced either. What follows is our summary; the registries' own lists decide.

The rules for a new name

  1. One or two words. Two words that make one name are joined with a hyphen: Kök-Jal, Boz-Bee. At most 18 letters, the hyphen not counted.
  2. Letters only. The Iradi Latin letters, with ö, ü, ı and ñ. No figures, no Roman numerals (a second Jaltyrak is not Jaltyrak II), no full stops or apostrophes.
  3. Not in use. A name cannot be given again while a horse of that name foaled in the last 25 years is in either studbook.
  4. Never again. The names on our famous horses page are closed for good, by custom where the registries have no rule: nobody names a foal Almanbet or Jel-Kayyk.
  5. No people, firms or goods. The name of a living person needs that person's written consent; names of firms, brands and products are refused (the Chongtor Registry turned down Aigyr for a sponsor's colt in 2019, and Jackpot the year after).
  6. Iradi, if you can. Foreign names are accepted when spelt in Iradi letters, but the inspectors' committee has been known to ask why.

How the old names were made

Most names describe the horse: a colour word and a second word, for the coat, a mark, a gait or a hope. ak white, kara black, boz grey, kök blue-grey, sary yellow, kyzyl red, chabdar roan, ala piebald; jal mane, tuyak hoof, kashka blaze, jel wind, bee mare. Many Akyel names are older words for speed and light (Jaltyrak, "gleaming"). The glossary has more.

Racing colours

Owners register their colours with the National Equestrian Sports Directorate, not with us. For a try at both, Jarysh lets you design silks and name a horse by these rules; the name check there reads this database.
